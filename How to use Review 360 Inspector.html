Review 360 Inspector

A helper that opens beside a Storyline course in Chrome. It shows you the alt text on every image and button, the closed captions, and the notes for the slide on screen, and lets you copy them, jump to any slide, and check knowledge checks.

You only need the course open in Chrome, from a Review 360 link or a published course. The publisher keeps the project file; this tool only reads the course already on your screen, and it never changes the published course.

Part 1 · Once per computer

Find Review 360 Inspector.zip in your Downloads. Right-click it, choose Extract All, then click Extract.

You get a folder named Review 360 Inspector with the tool's files and this guide inside. Move it somewhere it can stay, such as Documents. Chrome runs the tool from this folder, so don't delete it.

Updates every 2 seconds. Pauses while you type.
  1. 1Refresh. Looks for the course again. Use it after the course loads or if the panel seems stuck.
  2. 2Light or dark. Switches the panel between a light and a dark background.
  3. 3Status. A green dot and Connected to … means it found the course. It also counts the slides.
  4. 4Tabs. Click one to switch. The open tab is underlined in indigo.
  5. 5On screen. Always shows the slide the course is on, with its scene and slide number.
  6. 6Previous, Next, Reset slide. Move one slide back or forward. Reset slide puts only this slide back the way it opened.
  7. 7Save a copy of this view. Downloads a text file with the slide list, knowledge checks, and this slide's alt text, notes, and captions.
It keeps itself up to date The panel follows the course: when the slide changes, every tab updates within two seconds. You never need to copy anything into it.
Reset slide is the only button that changes anything And only the slide on screen, only until you reload the page. Reloading the browser tab puts the whole course back. The published course file is never edited.

Part 4 · The tabs

Alt text

Alt text is the description a screen reader speaks for an image or a button. This tab lists the images and buttons on the slide, top to bottom, with exactly what a screen reader gets, and flags anything to fix.

  1. 1Images and buttons only is on by default. Turn it off to also see text, shapes, and answer choices.
  2. 2The count tells you how many items need attention on this slide.
  3. 3Click a card to draw a box around that object on the slide. The card gets an indigo outline. Click it again to clear.

An image with no alt text shows its file name, in grey, so you can find it in the project. Player controls and anything hidden or invisible are left out.

Part 4 · The tabs

Captions and notes

Storyline won't let you copy captions or notes. These two tabs show them as plain text for the slide on screen, with copy buttons, and you never have to play the slide.

Captions

  1. 1Copy captions copies the text, one caption per line, without the times.
  2. 2Each line shows when it starts. Captions on another layer (feedback, for example) are listed under that layer's name.

Caption files imported as SRT, VTT, or SBV all show here. "This slide has no closed captions" means just that.

Notes Copy notes
A target audience is a group of people with certain demographics and behavior. Demographics could include age, occupation, disease conditions, and more.

Before you seek to raise awareness of your program, it's important to map out your target audience and their needs.

Notes

  1. 3Copy notes copies the slide's notes as plain text, one line per paragraph.

These are the same notes the course shows in its Notes tab (some courses call it Transcript).

Quick review routine Open a slide, then glance through Alt text, Captions, and Notes. Copy what you need into your review notes, press Next in the slide bar, and repeat.

  • Change, save, or upload the published course
